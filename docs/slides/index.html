DFM — fact ORDER ITEMgrain: one line of an order · 112,650 instances

Measures: price, freight value, freight ratio (= freight / price), distance seller → customer in km (haversine on the median coordinates of the two zip prefixes).
Dimensions: purchase date, customer, product, seller, order status, order (line number as descriptive attribute). Four hierarchies with 3–5 levels.
Data Management 2025/26 — Olist Data Warehouse5

DFM — fact ORDERgrain: one order · 99,441 instances

Why two facts

Delivery time, delay and review score are properties of the order. On the line fact they would be repeated on every line of a multi-item order (9,803 orders): an average review by category would count an order with three items three times.

Each measure lives at the grain where it exists. The two facts share the same conformed dimensions (date, customer, status) and are combined by drill-across on order_id.

Notation

Date is a role-playing dimension (purchase, delivered, promised). Delivered date is an optional arc: 2,965 orders have none.

Data Management 2025/26 — Olist Data Warehouse6

Logical model: star schemasurrogate keys, hierarchies denormalised in the dimension tables, foreign keys declared and indexed · compact view, full schema in docs/dfm/star_schema.svg

Star, not snowflake. One join per dimension, group by the level you need — no chain to reach "region". Dimensions are small (96k, 33k, 3k, 800 rows): repeating "Southeast" costs a few hundred kB. Hierarchies are strict and loaded once: no update anomaly.
When a snowflake would pay off: a shared, independently maintained dim_geography, or large dimensions. Neither applies here. order_id is a degenerate dimension in both facts; status_key is replicated on the line fact — a key, not a measure, so no additivity issue.

ETL: four of the twelve problems, and their SQLall in the reconciled layer; the full list with numbers is in docs/DESIGN.md §3

1 · One review per order

The natural key of a review in this dataset is the order, not review_id. Keep the most recent one.

SELECT DISTINCT ON (order_id)
       order_id, review_id, review_score::INT …
FROM staging.order_reviews
ORDER BY order_id,
         review_answer_timestamp DESC NULLS LAST,
         review_creation_date DESC;

2 · The customer is a person

dim_customer is built on customer_unique_id; the address is the one of the most recent order. A map table keeps the per-order key for the facts.

3 · One point per zip prefix

Bounding box on Brazil, most frequent (state, city) per prefix, median latitude and longitude — then the distance seller → customer for every line:

2 * 6371 * asin(sqrt(
    sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)·cos(lat₂)·sin²(Δlng/2)))

4 · Hierarchies that do not exist in the files

State → region from IBGE (27 rows). Category → macro-category: 73 → 12, defined by hand (home_confort and home_comfort_2 are two Olist categories). Translation file completed; NULL category → unknown, its own macro-category.

Quality, performance, reproducibility

Quality checks — run at the end of every build

  • row counts of facts and dimensions = sources (nothing lost, nothing duplicated)
  • the two facts agree: Σ total_price = Σ price, same for freight
  • n_items per order = number of lines; is_late consistent with delay_days
  • dim_date has no gaps; every review flag has a score

A failure raises an exception and stops run_all.sh.

Additivity, respected in every query

Money and counts are summed; days, distances, ratios are averaged; the review score is a judgement — average and distribution, never a sum.

Conclusions and limits

What was built

  • nine files + two added sources integrated through a reconciled layer written in SQL, twelve data-quality problems handled explicitly
  • DFM with two facts at their own grain and star schema on conformed dimensions, choice motivated against the snowflake
  • four hierarchies with 3–5 levels; role-playing and optional dimensions
  • seven OLAP sessions — roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice, pivot, drill-across — each with a finding
  • quality checks, materialised views, one-command rebuild

The finding worth remembering

Customers do not reward early delivery and punish lateness hard: one week late and the modal review is one star, in every region alike. Distance drives it, and the promised date absorbs most of it.

Limits

  • coordinates are medians per 5-digit zip prefix: distances are approximate (±10–20 km in cities); 555 lines have none
  • revenue = item prices; total_paid from payments differs slightly on a few orders (vouchers, rounding) and is kept apart
  • a sample of two years with partial ends: 20 months are comparable across years
  • the macro-categories are our grouping, not Olist's

Possible extensions

  • a customer-level fact (repeat purchases, lifetime value) — 2,997 repeat buyers is thin
  • slowly changing dimension for sellers if the data covered more years
  • text of the reviews as a further source
