2 Data Management 2025/26 · Paolo Pagliarini

Task and data

  • Task: integrate the nine files of the Olist marketplace into one source for the analysis of sales, delivery performance, reviews and freight costs
  • Data: Olist Brazilian E-Commerce (Kaggle): 99,441 orders, 112,650 order lines, 3,095 sellers, Sept 2016 to Oct 2018. Only Jan 2017 to Aug 2018 is complete
  • Two sources added: the IBGE macro-regions (27 states → 5 regions) and 12 macro-categories defined by us over the 73 Olist categories. They are the top levels of the geographic and product hierarchies
  • Stack: PostgreSQL 14 and plain SQL for every transformation. Python is used only to copy the CSV files into the database and to draw the charts
  • Reproducible: a single script rebuilds everything from the raw files in about 10 seconds and runs the quality checks at the end

5 Data Management 2025/26 · Paolo Pagliarini

[ETL] Four fixes in the reconciled layer

One review per order

The key of a review is the order, not review_id. The most recent one is kept: DISTINCT ON (order_id) ordered by answer time. The average of two different scores would be a score nobody gave.

The customer is a person

dim_customer is built on customer_unique_id (96,096 people), with the address of the most recent order. A map table keeps the per-order id for loading the facts.

One point per zip prefix

Samples outside Brazil dropped, most frequent state and city, median latitude and longitude, so a few wrong samples do not move the point. The seller → customer distance is the haversine between the two points.

Hierarchies that are not in the files

State → region from IBGE (27 rows). Category → macro-category, 73 → 12, defined by hand. Translation file completed; products without category go to unknown.

7 Data Management 2025/26 · Paolo Pagliarini

[DFM] Fact ORDER, and why two facts

Grain: one order · 99,441 instances. One date hierarchy shared by three roles (double circle); the dash marks the optional delivered date (2,965 orders have none).

  • Delivery, delay and review belong to the order, and 9,803 orders have more than one line
  • Order A has 3 lines and review 1, order B one line and review 5: per order (1+5)/2 = 3; copied on the lines (1+1+1+5)/4 = 2
  • On the data: average review 4.09 at order grain, 4.03 at line grain (single-line orders 4.16, orders with 3+ lines 3.43)
  • The two facts share conformed dimensions (date, customer, status) and are combined by drill-across on order_id.
8 Data Management 2025/26 · Paolo Pagliarini

[STAR] Logical model: star schema

Generated from the PostgreSQL catalog: one line per foreign key, crow's foot on the many side. Compact view; every column is in docs/dfm/star_schema.svg.

  • Star rather than snowflake: one join per dimension, all levels in one table. The dimensions are small (96k, 33k, 3k and 800 rows) and loaded once, so repeating the upper levels costs little
  • A snowflake would make sense with large dimensions, or with a separate geography table shared by customers and sellers

Slice to the complete months (Jan 2017 to Aug 2018), month × year pivot, drill-down to the day.

[OLAP] Late delivery and reviews

Delivered orders with a review; delay = delivered date − promised date, then dice by region and by quarter.

  • From "on the promised day" (4.03) to "up to one week late" (2.71) the average drops by 1.3 stars, while arriving two weeks early instead of a few days early changes almost nothing (4.32 vs 4.27)
  • The on-time vs late penalty is about 2 stars in every region (1.93–2.07), while the late rate goes from 5.8 % (South) to 12.5 % (Northeast)
  • The worst quarter is 2018-Q1, right after Black Friday and Christmas: 12.7 % late, average review 3.94

[OLAP] Freight burden and seller concentration

CUBE macro-category × distance band on the line fact; ranking and cumulative share of the sellers with window functions.

  • Freight is 17.6 % of revenue overall: 12.7 % under 100 km, 21.6 % over 1000 km, 31.6 % for Home & Furniture over 1000 km

  • Concentration is highest in Fashion (top 3 sellers = 36.5 %) and lowest in Sports & Leisure (12.9 %)

Quality checks and materialised views

Checks at the end of every build

Row counts of facts and dimensions equal the sources; the two facts agree on totals (Σ total_price = Σ price); n_items equals the number of lines; is_late is consistent with delay_days; the calendar has no gaps. A violation raises an exception and stops the build.

Additivity respected in every query

Money and counts are summed; days, distances and ratios are averaged; the review is a judgement, so average or distribution, never a sum.

Materialised viewRows
month × product hierarchy × state × status13,417
seller region × customer region × year56
  • Revenue by macro-category for one month: 110 ms on the line fact, under 1 ms on the view

Takeaways

  • [ETL] Nine files and two added sources integrated in a reconciled layer written in SQL; twelve data-quality problems handled explicitly
  • [DFM] Two facts, each at the grain where its measures exist, on conformed dimensions
  • [STAR] Star schema with one join per dimension; the choice is motivated against the snowflake
  • [OLAP] Seven sessions (roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice, pivot, drill-across), each with a finding
  • Main result: reviews barely change for early deliveries and drop by about two stars for late ones, in every region; late deliveries become more frequent as the distance grows
  • Limits: distances are approximate (median point of a 5-digit zip prefix); two years with incomplete ends; the macro-categories are our grouping
